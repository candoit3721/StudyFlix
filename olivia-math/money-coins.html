Chapter 1

Meet the Canadian Coins (Drawn to Real Size)

Every coin below is drawn at its true relative size, so the biggest surprise in Canadian money is impossible to miss.

🪙 The Dime Trap: The dime (10¢) is the smallest coin in your hand, but it is worth twice the much bigger nickel (5¢). Size does not equal value!
🍁 Shape & Metal Clues: The loonie ($1) is gold and has 11 straight sides you can feel. The toonie ($2) is the only coin made of two metals: a silver ring around a gold centre.
Chapter 2

Writing Money Two Ways: Cents and Dollars

The same amount can be written with a cent sign or a dollar sign. 100 cents = $1.00.

  • 75¢ = $0.75 (less than a dollar, so a zero goes in front of the dot)
  • 275¢ = $2.75 (two whole dollars and 75 cents left over)
  • $3.05 means 3 dollars and 5 cents, not 3 dollars and 50 cents. Always write two digits after the dot!
✏️ The Split Rule: To turn cents into dollars, split the number two digits from the right: 4 | 85 → $4.85.
Chapter 3

Counting a Pile: Biggest Coin First

Cashiers never count coins in a random order. Sort from the biggest value down, then skip count.

🧮 Skip-Counting Ladders: Quarters go 25, 50, 75, 100. Dimes go 10, 20, 30. Nickels go 5, 10, 15. Four quarters make exactly one loonie.
Chapter 4

Making Change: The "Count Up" Ladder

Do not subtract in your head. Start at the price and hop up to what you paid. The hops are the change.

Chapter 5

Canada Has No Pennies: The Cash Rounding Rule

The penny was retired in 2013. When you pay cash, the final total rounds to the nearest 5 cents. Paying by debit or credit card still charges the exact amount.

Total ends inRoundsExample
1¢ or 2¢DOWN to 0$7.32 → $7.30
3¢ or 4¢UP to 5$7.34 → $7.35
6¢ or 7¢DOWN to 5$7.37 → $7.35
8¢ or 9¢UP to 10$7.38 → $7.40
🍁 Fairness Check: Rounding happens on the whole bill, once, at the very end. It never happens on each item, and it never happens on the tax.
