### Challenge 2: Mystery Cell Detective
1. **Plant Cell**
2. The two giveaway organelles are:
   * **Cell Wall** (gives the rigid rectangular box shape)
   * **Chloroplasts** (bright green solar discs for photosynthesis)
   * *(Bonus: Giant central vacuole)*

### Challenge 3: Think Like a Biologist!
* *Scientific Explanation*: Plants do not have internal skeletons (bones) or muscles to support their weight. The rigid cellulose **cell wall** (and water pressure inside the large central vacuole) gives plant stems and trunks the structural strength to stand upright toward sunlight. Humans and animals have bony skeletal systems and muscle tissues instead, so our cells stay flexible to allow movement!

## 🎢 File 02 Answers: Forces, Energy & Motion

### Challenge 1: Roller Coaster Energy Check
1. **Point A** (highest point, stored gravitational potential energy is maximum).
2. **Point C** (lowest point at bottom of hill, where all potential energy has converted into kinetic motion energy).
3. **A) Potential energy is turning into Kinetic energy** as gravity accelerates the coaster downward.

### Challenge 2: Balanced vs. Unbalanced Forces
1. **Balanced** forces ($100\text{N} = 100\text{N}$).
2. The rope will **stay perfectly still** (net force is 0).
3. When Team Red pulls with $150\text{N}$, the forces become **Unbalanced** (Net force = $50\text{N}$ to the right). The rope will accelerate towards **Team Red**.

### Challenge 3: Newton's Law Match-Up
1. Balloon flying forward as air rushes backward: **Newton's 3rd Law** (Action & Reaction).
2. Heavier soccer ball needing more force: **Newton's 2nd Law** ($F = m \times a$).
3. Passengers lurching forward when bus brakes: **Newton's 1st Law** (Inertia).

## 🕵️‍♀️ File 03 Answers: Scientific Method Detective

### Challenge 1: Slime Bounce Test
1. **Independent Variable (IV)**: The slime recipe ingredients (Batch A standard vs Batch B cornstarch vs Batch C shaving cream).
2. **Dependent Variable (DV)**: The bounce height measured in centimeters ($\text{cm}$).
3. **Controlled Variables (Constants)**:
   * Same drop height ($1\text{ meter}$).
   * Same type of floor surface (tile floor).
   * Same size / mass of the slime ball.
   * Same room temperature and dropping method (no throwing downward).

### Challenge 2: Spot the Flawed Experiment!
* *Detective Analysis*: Leo changed too many variables at the same time, making it an **unfair test**:
  1. The runners were completely different ages and athletic abilities (16-year-old athlete vs 7-year-old cousin).
  2. The track surfaces were different (dry rubber track vs muddy grass).
  3. The weather conditions were different (dry vs raining).
* *To fix it*: Leo should have the **SAME runner** run on the **SAME track** under the **SAME weather**, changing ONLY the shoes!
