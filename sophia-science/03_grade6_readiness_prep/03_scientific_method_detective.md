## 🔍 1. The 3 Golden Variables (The "I-D-C" Rule!)

Whenever you run a science experiment, you must keep it a **Fair Test**:

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                        THE 3 VARIABLES FORMULA                         │
│                                                                        │
│  1. 🎛️ INDEPENDENT VARIABLE (IV): The ONE thing "I" change on purpose!  │
│  2. 📏 DEPENDENT VARIABLE (DV): What I MEASURE (the data/result).      │
│  3. 🔒 CONTROLLED VARIABLES: Everything kept EXACTLY THE SAME!         │
└────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

* **Independent Variable (IV)**: *"What am I testing?"*  
  *(Example: Testing different liquids to see which makes plants grow tallest $\rightarrow$ Liquid type is the IV).*
* **Dependent Variable (DV)**: *"What am I measuring?"*  
  *(Example: The height of the plant in centimeters $\rightarrow$ Plant height is the DV).*
* **Controlled Variables (Constants)**: Everything else you keep identical so the test is fair!  
  *(Example: Same type of seed, same amount of sunlight, same pot, same room temperature).*

## 🧐 2. The Scientific Method Cycle

1. **Ask a Question**: *"Does cold water freeze faster than hot water?"*
2. **Form a Hypothesis**: Use the **"If... then... because..."** template!  
   * *"IF I put hot water and cold water in the freezer, THEN cold water will freeze first, BECAUSE it starts closer to freezing temperature ($0^\circ\text{C}$)."*
3. **Design a Fair Experiment**: Change ONLY the IV!
4. **Collect & Record Data**: Use tables, charts, and metric units ($\text{cm}$, $\text{mL}$, $\text{g}$).
5. **Draw Conclusions**: Was your hypothesis supported or not? (In science, being wrong is totally fine — you still learned something new!).

## 👩‍🔬 3. Bonus Fun Kitchen Mini-Labs for Sophia!

### Mini-Lab #1: The Magic Penny Surface Tension Challenge 🪙
* **Question**: How many drops of water can fit on top of a single coin before spilling?
* **What you need**: A clean penny, a dropper or straw, water, and paper towel.
* **Science Mystery**: Water molecules love sticking to each other (called **Cohesion** and **Surface Tension**), creating a giant dome bubble on top of the coin!
* **Hypothesis**: I predict `_____` drops will fit.
* **Actual Result**: `_____` drops!

---

### Mini-Lab #2: Dancing Raisins (Gas Bubble Lift) 🍇🫧
* **What you need**: A clear glass, carbonated soda (Sprite/Club Soda) or (water + 1 tsp baking soda + 1 tsp vinegar), and 5 raisins.
* **What happens**: Drop the raisins in. Carbon dioxide ($CO_2$) gas bubbles stick to the wrinkly raisin skin like tiny life jackets, lifting them to the surface. When the bubbles pop at the top, the raisins sink down again!
