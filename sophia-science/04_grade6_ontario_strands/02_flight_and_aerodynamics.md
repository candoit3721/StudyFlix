## 📖 Part 1: Real-World Context & Concept Spotlight

A Canadair CL-415 water bomber drops toward a burning forest in northern Ontario.
It skims a lake at 130 km/h, scoops 6 137 L of water in twelve seconds, and climbs away.
That water has a mass of 6 137 kg, so the aircraft suddenly weighs about 60 000 N more than it did a moment earlier.
The wings have to find that much extra lift, immediately.
Understanding how is the whole of this strand.

### 💡 Concept 1: Air Is Not Nothing

Air has four properties that make flight possible.

1. Air has **mass**. The air in an average classroom masses roughly 60 kg.
2. Air takes up **space**. That is why an inflated basketball is firm.
3. Air can be **compressed**. That is how a bicycle pump works.
4. Air exerts **pressure**. About 101 kPa at sea level, pushing in every direction.

### 💡 Concept 2: The Four Forces, in Two Opposing Pairs

```
                    LIFT (up)
                      ▲
                      │
      DRAG  ◀─────── ✈ ───────▶  THRUST
      (backward)      │           (forward)
                      ▼
                   WEIGHT (down)

    VERTICAL PAIR:    lift  vs  weight   →  climb, level or descend
    HORIZONTAL PAIR:  thrust vs drag     →  speed up, steady or slow down
```

**Balanced** does not mean zero. It means equal and opposite.
Straight and level flight at a steady speed means **both** pairs are balanced.

| If... | Then the aircraft... |
|---|---|
| Lift > Weight | Climbs |
| Lift < Weight | Descends |
| Thrust > Drag | Speeds up |
| Thrust < Drag | Slows down |

### 💡 Concept 3: Bernoulli's Principle Makes Lift

> **Where air moves faster, its pressure drops.**

A wing is curved more on top than underneath.
Air crossing the upper surface has further to travel, so it speeds up, so its pressure falls.
Higher pressure underneath then pushes the wing upward.

```
        faster air  →  LOWER pressure
   ───────────────────────────────────
    −    −    −    −    −    −    −
        ╭─────────────────────╮
   ────╯                       ╰────      the aerofoil
        ╰─────────────────────╯
    +    +    +    +    +    +    +
   ───────────────────────────────────
        slower air  →  HIGHER pressure
              net push = LIFT
```

### 💡 Concept 4: Angle of Attack and the Stall

Raising the nose increases lift, but only up to about **15 degrees**.
Past that critical angle the air can no longer follow the curved upper surface.
It separates into turbulence, the speed difference disappears, and lift collapses.
That is a **stall**. More angle is not always more lift.

### 💡 Concept 5: Wing Loading

$$\text{wing loading} = \frac{\text{weight}}{\text{wing area}}$$

Low wing loading means slow, gentle, manoeuvrable flight.
High wing loading means fast flight and a long runway.

| Flyer | Mass | Wing area | Wing loading |
|---|---|---|---|
| Monarch butterfly | 0.5 g | 11 cm² | about 4 N/m² |
| Little brown bat | 8 g | 0.012 m² | about 7 N/m² |
| Canada goose | 4.5 kg | 0.22 m² | about 201 N/m² |
| Canadair CL-415 | 19 890 kg | 100.3 m² | about 1 945 N/m² |
| Dash 8 Q400 | 29 257 kg | 63.1 m² | about 4 549 N/m² |

### 💡 Concept 6: Adaptations for Flight

Living things and machines solve the same problem: keep **weight** low and make **lift** efficient.

* **Canada goose:** hollow bones, overlapping feathers, a deep keel bone for flight muscles, and V formation flying so each bird rides the upwash of the one ahead.
* **Little brown bat:** a skin membrane over long finger bones, reshaped mid-beat for turning no aircraft can match.
* **Aircraft:** aluminium and carbon fibre instead of steel, flaps to change wing shape for landing.
