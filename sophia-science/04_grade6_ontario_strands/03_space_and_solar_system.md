## 📖 Part 1: Real-World Context & Concept Spotlight

In 2001 the Space Shuttle carried a Canadian-built robotic arm into orbit.
Canadarm2 is 17.6 m long, has seven joints, and can move a 116 000 kg module with centimetre precision.
It built most of the International Space Station, and it can walk end over end across the station's hull.
Canada is the country that put the arm on the space station, and Canadian astronauts Chris Hadfield, Roberta Bondar and Jeremy Hansen have all flown because of it.

### 💡 Concept 1: Rotation Against Revolution

These two words are constantly confused. They are not the same motion.

| | Rotation | Revolution |
|---|---|---|
| What it is | Spinning on its own axis | Travelling around the Sun |
| How long for Earth | 24 hours | 365.25 days |
| What it causes | **Day and night** | **One year** |

### 💡 Concept 2: The Seasons Come From Tilt, Not Distance

This is the single biggest misconception in Grade 6 science.
Earth is actually **closest** to the Sun in early January, in the middle of a Canadian winter.

Earth's axis is tilted **23.5 degrees** and always points the same way in space.

```
        JUNE                                    DECEMBER
    north leans TOWARD Sun                north leans AWAY from Sun

         \  N                                    \  N
          \ │                                     \ │
     ☀     \│    Earth                  Earth      \│           ☀
           /│                                      /│
          / │                                     / │
         /  S                                    /  S

    Toronto: 15.4 h daylight              Toronto: 8.9 h daylight
    sun high in the sky                   sun low in the sky
    SUMMER                                WINTER
```

Direct sunlight spreads its energy over a small area, so it heats strongly.
Slanted sunlight spreads the same energy over a much bigger area, so it heats weakly.

### 💡 Concept 3: Mass Against Weight

| | Mass | Weight |
|---|---|---|
| What it measures | Amount of matter | Force of gravity on that matter |
| Unit | kilogram (kg) | newton (N) |
| Changes with location? | **Never** | **Yes** |
| Formula | not applicable | $W = m \times g$ |

| World | $g$ (N/kg) | Weight of a 40 kg student |
|---|---|---|
| The Moon | 1.62 | 65 N |
| Mars | 3.7 | 148 N |
| Mercury | 3.7 | 148 N |
| Venus | 8.9 | 356 N |
| Uranus | 8.9 | 356 N |
| **Earth** | **9.81** | **392 N** |
| Saturn | 10.4 | 416 N |
| Neptune | 11.2 | 448 N |
| Jupiter | 24.8 | 992 N |

### 💡 Concept 4: The Solar System's Components

| Planet | Diameter | Distance from Sun | One year | One day |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mercury | 4 879 km | 57.9 M km | 88 days | 176 Earth days |
| Venus | 12 104 km | 108.2 M km | 225 days | 243 Earth days |
| Earth | 12 756 km | 149.6 M km | 365.25 days | 24 hours |
| Mars | 6 792 km | 227.9 M km | 687 days | 24.6 hours |
| Jupiter | 142 984 km | 778.6 M km | 11.9 years | 9.9 hours |
| Saturn | 120 536 km | 1 433.5 M km | 29.5 years | 10.7 hours |
| Uranus | 51 118 km | 2 872.5 M km | 84 years | 17.2 hours |
| Neptune | 49 528 km | 4 495.1 M km | 164.8 years | 16.1 hours |

**The smaller bodies:**

* **Comet:** ice, dust and rock. Near the Sun it grows a tail that always points **away** from the Sun, because the solar wind blows it outward.
* **Asteroid:** rock and metal, mostly in the belt between Mars and Jupiter.
* **Meteoroid → meteor → meteorite:** in space it is a meteoroid, burning in the atmosphere it is a meteor (shooting star), and if it lands the piece is a meteorite.
* **Natural satellite:** a moon. Earth has 1, Saturn has 146.

### 💡 Concept 5: Space Technologies

| Technology | What it does | Canadian link |
|---|---|---|
| **Telescope** | Collects light to see distant objects | David Dunlap Observatory, Ontario |
| **Spectroscope** | Splits light to reveal what a star is made of | Used to identify elements in stars |
| **Satellite** | Orbits Earth for imaging or communication | RADARSAT ice and flood monitoring |
| **GPS** | Times signals from many satellites to fix a position | In every Canadian phone |
| **Robotic arm** | Moves cargo and astronauts in orbit | Canadarm, Canadarm2, Dextre |
